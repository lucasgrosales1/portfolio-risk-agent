Logo

The mark

A monogram badge, not a generic initials-in-a-box: bold "WS" set in the display face, sat on a gold ledger tick — the same underline that marks a computed figure everywhere else in the product. The tick is what makes this mark ours and not a stock app icon.

Primary mark — full color, on paper

Reversed — on marine or any dark ground

Primary lockupmark + wordmark, for letterhead and document headers
Clear space & minimum sizeprotect the mark from crowding and from disappearing

Clear space on every side ≥ the height of the "W" stroke.
Nothing else — text, edges, photography — enters that margin.

Below 32px the gold tick disappears rather than smearing — use logo-mark-favicon.svg, the tickless variant, at 16–24px.

In contextthe mark as it actually sits in the app today
Don'tthe mark is one file, used unaltered — these are why
✕ Stretch

Never scale the two axes independently. The badge is always a perfect square.

✕ Recolor

The mark is marine, paper and gold — never a different brand palette or a gradient.

✕ Rotate

The mark sits level. Rotation reads as a placeholder, not a brand.

✕ Add effects

No drop shadows, bevels or glows. The system is flat, printed paper — not glassy UI chrome.

✕ Retype

Never re-set "WS" in a different typeface. Use the master SVG file, always.

✕ Float on photography

Never place the mark directly over a busy photo. Give it a solid paper or marine plate first.

Typography

Three faces, three jobs

A display face for headings, a body face for reading, and a mono face reserved for one thing only: anything computed. If a number appears on screen, it is set in IBM Plex Mono — that rule alone is most of what makes this system recognizable.

Voice & tone

Say the number, not the adjective

The design earns trust by showing its work — the copy has to match that, or the whole system rings false.

01

State the figure, not the feeling

"100% figures computed" beats "we're incredibly thorough." A number a reader can check outperforms an adjective they have to trust.

02

Name what's assumed

When a rate or return is an illustrative assumption, say so in the same sentence — never let a modeled number pass as a fact.

03

Decline out loud

When a product or plan doesn't fit, say why, specifically — "declined: risk tolerance below moderate," not a vague pass.

04

No urgency, no hype

No countdowns, no "act now." A fiduciary's voice is unhurried because the advice doesn't expire.

"The entire pitch is that the math is real and computed."— the project's own operating principle
